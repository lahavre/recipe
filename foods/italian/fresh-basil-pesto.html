Fresh Basil Pesto

Bright, fragrant Genovese-style pesto — basil, pine nuts, garlic, Parmigiano, and good olive oil, blitzed in seconds. No cooking, and never any heat.

    Method

      Notes

      Dry the basil really well before blending, and blend only briefly — wet leaves and over-blending are what turn pesto brown and dull.

      Too thick? Drizzle in a little more olive oil and pulse again until it's the consistency you like.

      Keep pesto away from direct heat. To serve with pasta, toss it through off the heat with a splash of pasta water rather than cooking it in the pan.

      Store in a tightly sealed jar in the fridge with a thin layer of olive oil on top to stop it browning, and use within 3–4 days. It also freezes well in small containers or ice cube trays.

      Great with pasta or gnocchi, spread on focaccia, spooned over burrata, or alongside chicken, lamb, fish, or vegetables. A mortar and pestle is the traditional alternative to a blender.

      Recipe adapted from Vincenzo's Plate (also on YouTube). Pesto-per-portion guide from Sharmis Passions (4 tbsp pesto for 200g spaghetti).
